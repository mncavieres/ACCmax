ACCmax Workbench

Maximal systolic acceleration and acceleration time from spectral Doppler

Measures maximal systolic acceleration (ACCmax) and acceleration time (AT, or PAT in a pedal artery) from a spectral Doppler recording. Load a recording, run the automatic fit, then check the calipers on the stacked beats and move them if you disagree. Everything runs in your browser; files are not uploaded.

See the automatic fits on every available case, with checkplots for real recordings and simulations.

      How the fit works

      1. Envelope. The outer (maximum-velocity) edge of the spectrum is traced column by column, or read from a CSV.
      2. Period. A multi-harmonic Lomb–Scargle periodogram over the selected window gives the heart rate. It tolerates gaps such as the erase bar.
      3. Beats. Each systolic upstroke is found by stepping one period from the previous one. Each beat is then shifted to best match the average of the other beats (leave-one-out cross-correlation), so heart-rate variability does not smear the stack.
      4. Stack. The aligned beats are folded onto one time axis in milliseconds from the upstroke and smoothed with robust local quadratic regression.
      5. Landmarks. ACCmax is the steepest chord of the chosen span on the upstroke. The valley is the end-diastolic minimum found by walking back from that chord, so a reverse-flow trough is never used. AT runs from the onset to the first systolic peak.
      6. Uncertainty. Beats are resampled with replacement to give 68% and 95% intervals. These reflect beat-to-beat variation only, not angle or calibration error.
